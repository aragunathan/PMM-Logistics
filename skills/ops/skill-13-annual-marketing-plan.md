- Events and conferences

### 2A — Marketing Pipeline Target Framework

Formula to calculate marketing pipeline target:

Step 1: Get the total sales pipeline target for the year (from Sales)
Step 2: Decide what % marketing is responsible for sourcing
  - Typical for enterprise sales-led with strong ABM: 30% to 50%
  - Conservative starting point for Ramco year 1 of this system: 35%
Step 3: Calculate required marketing-sourced pipeline in USD
  Example: $10M total pipeline target × 35% = $3.5M marketing-sourced

Step 4: Work backwards to required activity volume

Conversion funnel assumptions (adjust based on actual data):
- Impressions / Reach → Website visit: 2% to 5%
- Website visit → Lead (form fill or demo request): 3% to 8%
- Lead → Sales Qualified Lead (SQL): 20% to 35%
- SQL → Opportunity (deal created in HubSpot): 60% to 75%
- Opportunity → Closed Won: 15% to 25% (enterprise, 180+ day cycle)
- Average deal size: $150,000+

Example backward calculation for $3.5M pipeline:
- Need: 23 closed won deals ($3.5M ÷ $150k avg ACV)
- Need: ~100 opportunities (at 23% close rate)
- Need: ~150 SQLs (at 67% SQL-to-opp rate)
- Need: ~500 leads (at 30% lead-to-SQL rate)
- Need: ~10,000 quality website visits (at 5% visit-to-lead rate)

### 5B — Event Investment Framework

For each Tier 1 event, calculate ROI before committing:

Required pipeline to justify event cost:
- Sponsorship + travel + team = total event cost
- Pipeline needed to break even = event cost ÷ (win rate × avg ACV)
- Example: $30,000 event cost ÷ (20% win rate × $150,000 ACV) = 1 closed deal
- Minimum target: 3× event cost in pipeline created within 90 days of event

Pre-event preparation (run event prep agent 4 to 6 weeks before each event):
Command: "Run the event prep agent for [EVENT NAME] in [REGION]"

Post-event pipeline tracking:
- Tag all event contacts in HubSpot with source = [Event Name] [Year]
- Review pipeline from each event at 30, 60, and 90 days post-event
- Decision rule: if event produces less than 2× cost in pipeline by 90 days,
  downgrade to Tier 2 or drop for next year

### 8A — Quarterly Competitive Review Cadence

Run a structured competitive review every quarter.
Own: Head of Marketing. Attend: Marketing lead + Sales lead + Product (optional).

What to review each quarter:

Competitor Activity Review:
- Have any competitors launched new products or features?
- Have any competitors entered new regions (AU, ID, SA, PH)?
- Have any competitors changed pricing or packaging?
- Have any competitors published thought leadership that gained traction?
- Have any competitors won notable 3PL customers we were also targeting?

Win/Loss Review:
- Review all deals lost this quarter — what did we lose to and why?
- Review all deals won this quarter — what did we beat and why?
- Are there patterns? (e.g. consistently losing on price to a specific competitor
  in Indonesia, consistently winning on integration depth in Australia)

Battlecard Update Trigger:
Run the competitive intel agent (Mode A) to refresh or build battlecards when:
- A competitor is mentioned in 3+ lost deals in one quarter
- A competitor launches a major product update
- A new competitor appears in a target region for the first time

Command: "Run the competitive intel agent for [COMPETITOR NAME]"

## FAILURE MODES

Failure 1: Plan is written but not connected to HubSpot attribution
Result: You cannot prove which channels generated pipeline.
Budget decisions are based on gut feel, not data.
Fix: Before executing any element of the plan, ensure HubSpot source
tracking is set up for every channel. Every lead must have:
Original Source, Source Drill-down 1, and Campaign fields populated.

Failure 2: Annual plan is too ambitious for the team size
Result: Team is spread thin, nothing is done well, burnout by Q2.
Fix: Prioritise ruthlessly. Pick the top 3 channels and top 3 initiatives.
Do fewer things better. The plan has 10 sections — not all 10 need to
be fully activated in year 1. Build sequentially.

Failure 3: Events are treated as brand activities with no pipeline accountability
Result: Events consume 25% of budget with no measurable pipeline output.
Fix: Every event must have a pipeline target before attending.
Run the event prep agent 4 to 6 weeks before every event.
Review pipeline at 30, 60, and 90 days post-event. Cut events that
do not produce 2× cost in pipeline within 90 days.

Failure 4: Organic SEO is deprioritised because it is slow
Result: No inbound pipeline from organic by end of year 2.
Fix: SEO is a 6 to 12 month investment. Start month 1.
Publish 2 blog posts per month consistently regardless of other activity.
The compounding effect of organic traffic means every month of delay
costs you pipeline in years 2 and 3.

Failure 5: Innovative programs have no success criteria
Result: Experimental budget is spent, no clear learning, no scale decision.
Fix: Every experiment in Section 9 must have:
- A 90-day window
- A numeric success criteria defined before starting
- A named owner
- A go/no-go decision date

Failure 6: Social media is inconsistent (post 10 times then nothing for a month)
Result: Algorithm deprioritises Ramco content. Follower growth stalls.
Zero compounding effect.
Fix: Create a 4-week content batch per month. Write and schedule
16 posts in one sitting per month. Never post reactively — always
from a plan. Consistency beats quality in the LinkedIn algorithm.
