## The Role of SEO Content in Ramco's GTM

At a 180+ day enterprise sales cycle, a prospect researching logistics software solutions may spend weeks or months reading content before they ever speak to a vendor. SEO content places Ramco in front of that research phase — at the moment a prospect is actively trying to understand their problem, evaluate options, or build an internal business case.

**What SEO content does for Ramco:**
- Captures in-market buyers at the research stage, before competitors reach them through outbound
- Builds domain authority that compounds over time — content published today generates leads for years
- Provides sales reps with assets to share that feel like independent research rather than vendor marketing
- Feeds inbound leads into HubSpot for nurture and SDR follow-up
- Supports LinkedIn paid campaigns as the content offer behind lead gen forms

**What SEO content cannot do alone:**
- Generate enterprise pipeline on its own — it is a top-of-funnel and mid-funnel tool; SDR and sales convert the leads
- Produce results in under 90 days — new content typically takes 3–6 months to rank; plan accordingly
- Replace outbound for T1 account acquisition — SEO catches inbound interest, outbound creates conversations with accounts that aren't searching yet

**Content volume target:** 4–6 articles per region per quarter = 20–30 articles published across all five regions per quarter. This is the minimum viable volume to build meaningful organic visibility within 12 months.

### STEP 1: Keyword Research by Region
**Time:** 3–4 hours (initial), 60 minutes/quarter (refresh) | **Owner:** Demand Gen / SEO Lead

Keyword research is the foundation. Every article must target a keyword that real 3PL decision-makers are searching for in that region. Do not publish content without a keyword target.

#### 1a: Seed Keyword Categories

Start with these seed categories — expand using your SEO tool in Step 1b.

**Category 1 — Software Solution Keywords (high commercial intent)**
These are prospects actively looking for a solution to buy or evaluate.
- "TMS software [country]" / "transport management system [country]"
- "WMS software [country]" / "warehouse management system [country]"
- "fleet management software [country]"
- "logistics software [country]"
- "3PL software [country]"
- "freight management software [country]"
- "supply chain management software [country]"

**Category 2 — Problem / Pain Keywords (high relevance, mid-funnel)**
These are prospects who know they have a problem and are researching it.
- "how to reduce billing disputes [logistics / 3PL]"
- "real-time shipment visibility software"
- "automated freight billing"
- "3PL onboarding new clients"
- "logistics platform integration ERP"
- "transport and warehouse management single platform"
- "cost per shipment reduction"

**Category 3 — Operational / Educational Keywords (top of funnel, awareness)**
These are prospects learning about the space — longer sales cycle but high volume.
- "what is a TMS" / "transport management system benefits"
- "3PL technology trends [year]"
- "logistics digitalisation [country]"
- "WMS vs TMS difference"
- "how 3PLs are using technology to scale"
- "supply chain visibility tools"

**Category 4 — Regional / Local Keywords (geography-specific)**
Prospects searching for solutions specific to their market.
- "logistics software Australia" / "TMS Australia"
- "sistem manajemen logistik Indonesia" (Indonesian — logistics management system)
- "برنامج إدارة النقل السعودية" (Arabic — transport management software Saudi)
- "logistics software Philippines" / "3PL system Philippines"

#### 1b: Keyword Research Process Using Ahrefs (or Semrush)

**For each region, run the following searches in your SEO tool:**

**Step 1:** Enter each seed keyword into Ahrefs Keywords Explorer. Set country filter to the target region.

**Step 2:** For each seed, pull:
- Search volume (monthly, local country)
- Keyword Difficulty (KD) score
- Top-ranking pages (to understand what Google wants to rank for this term)
- SERP features (does this keyword trigger a Featured Snippet, Local Pack, or People Also Ask box?)
- Parent topic (the broader topic this keyword belongs to — use to group related keywords into one article)

**Step 3:** Build a keyword opportunity matrix. For each keyword, score it:

| Score | KD | Volume | Commercial Intent |
|-------|-----|--------|-----------------|
| 5 | <20 | >100/mo | High (software, pricing, comparison) |
| 4 | 20–35 | 50–100/mo | High |
| 3 | 20–35 | >100/mo | Medium (how-to, benefits) |
| 2 | 35–50 | >200/mo | Medium |
| 1 | >50 | any | Low or unknown |

**Priority:**
- Score 5: Build immediately — low competition, high intent
- Score 4: Build in quarter 1 or 2
- Score 3: Build as volume content for authority
- Score 2: Build once scores 4 and 5 are covered
- Score 1: Ignore unless there is a strong strategic reason

**Step 4:** Group keywords into content clusters. A cluster is a pillar page (broad topic) + 4–8 supporting articles (specific subtopics). Building clusters, not isolated articles, is how you build topical authority and rank faster.

**Example cluster: "Logistics Software Australia"**
- Pillar: Logistics Software Australia — The Complete Guide for 3PLs
- Supporting article 1: TMS Software Australia — What to Look For in 2025
- Supporting article 2: WMS Software Australia — Top Features for 3PL Operators
- Supporting article 3: How Australian 3PLs Are Using Technology to Win Enterprise Contracts
- Supporting article 4: Logistics Software Integration with SAP — An Australian 3PL Guide
- Supporting article 5: Chain of Responsibility Compliance — How Technology Supports CoR Audits

All six pages link to each other. The pillar links to all supporting articles. Supporting articles link back to the pillar. This internal linking structure is what builds topical authority in Google's eyes.

### STEP 2: Competitive SERP Analysis
**Time:** 2 hours | **Owner:** SEO Lead / Demand Gen

Before writing any content brief, analyse the top 10 results for each priority keyword. This tells you what Google has decided deserves to rank — and therefore what your content must equal or exceed.

**For each target keyword, record:**

1. **Who is ranking?** — Are the top results other software vendors (SAP, Oracle, Infor, Körber), industry publications (Gartner, Logistics Management), directories (Capterra, G2), or informational blogs?

2. **What format is ranking?** — Is it a listicle, a how-to guide, a comparison page, a product page, a glossary entry? Match the format Google prefers for that intent.

3. **What is the average word count of the top 3 results?** — Your content must be at least as comprehensive. Use the free Chrome extension "SEO Meta in 1 Click" or Ahrefs to pull word counts quickly.

4. **What questions does the content answer?** — Pull the "People Also Ask" (PAA) boxes from the SERP. Every PAA question is a subheading or paragraph your content should include.

5. **What is missing from the existing results?** — This is your content differentiation. Typically, competitor content is: too generic (not specific to 3PLs), too old (pre-2024), not region-specific (written for a global audience), or too shallow (glosses over implementation and integration concerns). Ramco's content should fix every one of these gaps.

6. **Domain Authority of ranking pages** — If the top 10 results are all DA 80+ sites (G2, Gartner, Oracle), a new Ramco article will struggle to rank within 12 months unless you invest in link building (Step 6). Filter your keyword list to prioritise terms where mid-DA sites (DA 30–60) are in the top 10 — this signals Ramco can compete.

**Record findings in a SERP analysis spreadsheet:**
Columns: Keyword | Rank 1 URL | Rank 1 Domain | Rank 1 Word Count | Format | PAA Questions (top 3) | Content Gap | DA of Rank 1 | Ramco Opportunity Score (1–5)

**Time:** 60 minutes per brief | **Owner:** Demand Gen / SEO Lead (use Claude Prompt 1 to accelerate)

A content brief is the document that a writer receives before writing. It eliminates the back-and-forth of "this isn't what we needed" and ensures every published article is strategically aligned from the first draft.

### STEP 4: Content Production
**Time:** 3–5 hours per article (writing + review) | **Owner:** Writer (internal or agency) + Demand Gen review + Sales review

#### 4a: Writer Briefing

Before a writer starts:
1. Share the content brief (from Step 3)
2. Share the relevant persona section from `/inputs/persona-definitions.md` — the writer must understand who they are writing for
3. Share the approved messaging matrix from `skill-03` for the relevant persona — the writer must not contradict approved positioning
4. Share 2–3 competitor articles to beat — show what exists and what needs to be improved upon
5. Confirm the regional specifics — if the writer is not from the target region, they must not fabricate local context. They should flag gaps and the Demand Gen lead fills them in.

#### 4b: Content Quality Standards

Every article published under the Ramco brand must meet these standards before it goes live:

**Accuracy:**
- Every statistic cited must have a source link — no unverifiable claims ("studies show that 70%...")
- Every Ramco capability mentioned must be confirmed with the product team
- Regional regulatory references (CoR in AU, UU PDP in ID, PDPL in SA, DPA in PH) must be accurate and current

**Depth:**
- Must be at least as long as the top-ranking competitor for that keyword (based on SERP analysis)
- Must answer the primary question implied by the keyword fully — not superficially
- Must include at least one piece of content that does not exist in the competing articles (unique insight, regional data, specific 3PL scenario)

**Specificity:**
- Must be specific to 3PLs — not generic logistics or supply chain content
- Must include at least one region-specific reference (local regulation, local industry trend, named local association, or local market context)
- Must not read like it was written for a global audience and then a country name was dropped in

**On-Page SEO:**
- Primary keyword in: H1, first paragraph, at least one H2, meta title, meta description
- Secondary keywords distributed naturally throughout — not stuffed
- Internal links: minimum 2 links to other Ramco pages
- Image alt text: every image must have descriptive alt text including the primary keyword where natural
- Schema markup: Article schema on all blog posts (coordinate with Web team)

#### 4c: Review and Approval Process

1. **Writer submits first draft** → Demand Gen lead reviews for brief compliance, regional accuracy, and messaging alignment (24–48 hours)
2. **Demand Gen returns with edits** → Writer revises (24 hours)
3. **Regional Sales rep reviews** → Confirms regional accuracy and flags any claims that don't match real-world sales conversations (24 hours). This is a fast read — focus on: Does anything here contradict what we know about this market? Does anything sound wrong to a 3PL professional in this region?
4. **Demand Gen approves** → Submits to Web/CMS team for publishing
5. **Web team publishes** → Confirms URL, meta data, internal links, schema, and Insight Tag are all live before promotion begins

Total review cycle target: 5 business days from first draft submission to publish

### STEP 5: On-Page Optimisation and Technical SEO
**Time:** 30–45 minutes per article post-publish | **Owner:** Web Team / Technical SEO

After every article is published, complete this checklist before promoting:

#### On-Page Checklist
- [ ] H1 includes primary keyword — check the published page, not just the draft
- [ ] Meta title: under 60 characters, primary keyword in first 30 characters, no truncation in Google Search Console
- [ ] Meta description: under 155 characters, includes keyword, includes a benefit or reason to click
- [ ] First paragraph: primary keyword appears naturally in the first 100 words
- [ ] At least one H2 includes the primary keyword or a close variant
- [ ] All images have descriptive alt text
- [ ] Internal links point to: Ramco product page + at least one related article
- [ ] Schema markup: Article schema is applied (check via Google Rich Results Test — search.google.com/test/rich-results)
- [ ] Canonical tag: self-referencing canonical is set (prevents duplicate content issues)
- [ ] Page loads in under 3 seconds on mobile (test via Google PageSpeed Insights)

#### Post-Publish Technical Steps
1. Submit the new URL to Google Search Console for indexing: **Google Search Console → URL Inspection → Request Indexing**
2. Add the new article to the XML sitemap (confirm with Web team it is included automatically, or add manually)
3. Find 3–5 existing Ramco pages that are topically related and add an internal link to the new article from each — this passes authority to the new page and helps it rank faster
4. Set up a Google Search Console alert for the new URL to monitor when it first appears in search results

### STEP 6: Link Building (Off-Page SEO)
**Time:** 2–3 hours per month | **Owner:** Demand Gen / SEO Lead

Links from other websites to Ramco's content are the most powerful ranking signal in Google's algorithm. New content will rank faster and for more competitive keywords if it attracts quality backlinks. This is the step most B2B marketers skip — and it explains why their content never ranks.

**Link building tactics for Ramco's logistics content:**

**Tactic 1 — Industry Association Mentions**
Submit content to logistics association newsletters and resource pages in each region:
- Australia: LAA (laa.asn.au), SCLAA (sclaa.com.au) — offer a contributed article or resource mention
- Indonesia: ALFI (alfi.or.id) — submit industry content for their member resources
- Saudi Arabia: SAFLA — submit research or regulatory compliance content
- Philippines: SCMAP (scmap.org), PHILFREIGHT — offer contributed op-eds or research citations

Target: 2–3 industry association links per quarter. These are high-relevance, high-trust links.

**Tactic 2 — Contributed Articles (Guest Posts)**
Write original articles for logistics and supply chain publications that link back to Ramco's content:
- Global: Supply Chain Brain (supplychainbrain.com), Logistics Management (logisticsmgmt.com), Supply Chain Dive
- Australia: Australasian Transport News (atn.com.au), Logistics & Materials Handling
- Southeast Asia: Logistics Asia, Supply Chain Asia
- Target: 1 contributed article per quarter, per region (rotate focus)

**Tactic 3 — Resource Page Link Building**
Find pages on logistics association, university, or industry publication websites that list "useful logistics software resources" or "3PL technology guides." Use Ahrefs: search your competitor's domain for backlinks from resource pages. Reach out to the same pages with Ramco's content.

**Tactic 4 — Digital PR / Data-Led Content**
Commission or produce a piece of original data (a survey, an industry benchmark report, or a data analysis) specific to the logistics market in one region. Data-led content attracts links naturally because other publications cite it. Aim for one data-led piece per region per year.

**Tactic 5 — Broken Link Building**
Use Ahrefs to find pages on logistics-related websites that link to a broken URL (404 error). If Ramco has relevant content on the same topic, reach out to the site owner and offer your content as a replacement. This is a high-acceptance-rate outreach strategy because you are doing the webmaster a favour.

**Minimum monthly link building activity:** 5 outreach emails sent per region with a target of 1 link secured per region per month. Track all outreach in a Google Sheet: URL approached / contact name / date sent / date followed up / result.

#### Australia

**Language and tone:**
- Write in Australian English: "organisation" not "organization", "recognise" not "recognize", "labour" not "labor", "colour" not "color"
- Tone: Direct, no-nonsense, evidence-based. Australian 3PL professionals are sceptical of marketing-speak. Every claim must be backed with a source or an example.
- Avoid Americanisms in examples — don't reference US retail chains, US freight networks, or USD benchmarks without converting to AUD and Australian context

**Regional content angles that perform strongly:**
- Chain of Responsibility (CoR): "How Australian 3PLs Use Technology to Meet Chain of Responsibility Obligations" — this is a legally mandated compliance topic; content ranking for CoR terms attracts exactly the right CIO and Ops persona in Australia
- FMCG and retail logistics: Articles specific to 3PLs servicing Woolworths, Coles, Aldi, Kmart supply chains — these companies set strict compliance and visibility requirements that 3PLs must meet to win and retain contracts
- Interstate linehaul: Content on managing transport visibility and cost across Sydney–Melbourne–Brisbane–Perth corridors — extremely specific to Australian 3PL operations
- Carbon and sustainability: "How Australian 3PLs Are Reporting Scope 3 Logistics Emissions" — emerging compliance requirement with growing search volume
- Labour and automation: Australian logistics workforce shortages are well-documented; content on how technology offsets headcount dependency is directly relevant

**Local sources to cite (adds credibility and relevance signals to Google):**
- ABS (Australian Bureau of Statistics) — logistics and transport industry data
- Infrastructure Australia — freight infrastructure reports
- BITRE (Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics) — freight movement data
- LAA and SCLAA publications
- ACCC (Australian Competition & Consumer Commission) — for any content touching pricing or market concentration

**Pillar content priority for Australia:**
1. "Logistics Software Australia — The Complete Guide for 3PLs" (primary commercial pillar)
2. "Transport Management System Australia — What 3PLs Need to Know in 2025"
3. "Warehouse Management System Australia — A 3PL Buyer's Guide"
4. "Chain of Responsibility Compliance Software — How Australian 3PLs Use Technology to Stay Compliant"
5. "How Australian 3PLs Are Winning Enterprise Contracts with Digital Logistics Infrastructure"

## Failure Modes and How to Fix Them

| Failure Mode | Symptom | Fix |
|-------------|---------|-----|
| Content doesn't rank after 6 months | Articles indexed but sitting at position 50+ with no movement | Audit keyword difficulty — the target keyword may be too competitive for current domain authority; shift to longer-tail variants with lower KD; build backlinks to the non-ranking article |
| Generic content with no regional specificity | Low time-on-page (<60 seconds); high bounce rate; no ranking despite optimisation | Rewrite with mandatory regional specificity (regulation, local association reference, local market example); use the content audit prompt (Prompt 3) to diagnose and fix |
| Organic traffic but zero leads | Thousands of sessions, no HubSpot contacts created | Audit the CTA — is it present, is it compelling, is the offer relevant? Check that HubSpot tracking code is on the content pages; add an exit-intent or inline lead capture form |
| Articles indexed but not for target keyword | Ranking for unrelated keywords; correct keyword not in GSC impression data | Keyword stuffing or wrong intent match — rewrite H1, meta title, first paragraph, and at least one H2 to include the exact target keyword; check if the article content matches the search intent of the keyword |
| Machine-translated Bahasa Indonesia or Arabic content | Low rankings for Indonesian and Arabic keywords; no dwell time on those pages | Replace with native-language writer; machine translation content is penalised by Google's Helpful Content system; this is a complete rewrite, not an edit |
| No backlinks acquired | Domain authority stagnant; content not ranking despite good on-page optimisation | Begin structured link building outreach (Step 6); without backlinks, even perfect on-page SEO will underperform in competitive markets; prioritise industry association mentions first |
| Content production stalls | Quarter ends with fewer than 4 articles published | Brief queue is the constraint — build 3 content briefs in advance before the production cycle begins; never let the brief queue run dry; Prompt 1 generates a complete brief in under 10 minutes |
| Sales team doesn't use content | Articles published but SDRs never share them; no asset sharing in email sequences | Build a monthly "new content brief" for the sales team — title, one-line summary, target persona, and a ready-to-use email sentence; make sharing frictionless |
| Keyword cannibalisation | Two Ramco articles competing for the same keyword; neither ranks well | Merge the weaker article into the stronger one via a redirect (301); update the surviving article with the best content from both; update internal links to point to the surviving URL |
| HubSpot organic attribution broken | Organic leads showing as "Direct" in HubSpot | Check HubSpot tracking code is installed on all content pages; check the session cookie length matches the content journey (set to 30 days minimum); ensure HubSpot's JavaScript is loading before any other analytics scripts |
