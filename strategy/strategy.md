# Go-to-Market Strategy
## Ramco Systems — Logistics Software Division
**Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 2026-03-24 | **Owner:** Marketing Lead

---

## Executive Summary

Ramco's logistics software division is targeting $8M USD ARR in 2026 across four regions — Australia, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, and the Philippines — selling an integrated TMS, WMS, Fleet Management, Hub Management, and Rating & Billing platform to 3PL companies with $100M+ annual revenue.

The GTM motion is account-based marketing (ABM) combined with multi-stakeholder consensus selling. Average ACV is $150k+ (floor); the sales cycle runs 180+ days. With 21 active deals in pipeline and a 20% win rate, the programme is early-stage and channel-lean — operating on a $100K annual marketing budget.

**The single most important strategic challenge:** Price is the #1 loss reason, yet the product wins on features and integration depth. The strategy must front-load value and ROI justification before commercial conversations begin — shifting price from a deal-killer to a secondary consideration.

**The single most important growth lever:** Australia is the primary market. Organic search is already generating traction. The new TMS platform launch in 2026 is the anchor campaign event for the year.

## Market Opportunity

### Market Context
The Asia-Pacific 3PL market is growing driven by e-commerce expansion, supply chain modernisation, and increasing enterprise shipper expectations for digital capability. 3PLs with $100M+ annual revenue are under pressure from two sides: their enterprise customers demand real-time visibility and digital interfaces, and their competitors are investing in technology to differentiate on capability rather than price alone.

The four target regions represent distinct opportunity profiles:

| Region | Market Status | Maturity | Key Driver |
|--------|--------------|----------|------------|
| Australia | Primary | Active | E-commerce growth, CoR compliance, retail SLA demands |
| Indonesia | Active | Growing | Inter-island logistics complexity, e-commerce boom |
| Saudi Arabia | Early Stage | Emerging | Vision 2030 logistics modernisation mandate |
| Philippines | Active | Growing | Metro Manila last-mile, FMCG/pharma logistics |

### Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
- **Company type:** Third-party logistics (3PL) provider
- **Annual revenue:** $100M+ USD
- **Operational scope:** Multi-client, multi-modal — road freight, warehousing, or both
- **Technology trigger:** Running disconnected TMS + WMS + billing systems; approaching a platform consolidation decision or contract renewal
- **Buying trigger:** Losing deals to more digitally capable competitors, billing revenue leakage, inability to scale without headcount growth, or a compliance event (CoR, PDPL, ZATCA)
- **Deal size:** $150K ACV minimum; target $300K+ for enterprise accounts

### Total Addressable Market (TAM)
Estimated 400–600 3PL companies with $100M+ annual revenue across Australia, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, and the Philippines. Not all are currently in a buying cycle; the ABM programme targets the subset in active evaluation or approaching a renewal decision.

### Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)
Tier 1 and Tier 2 accounts on the target account list — companies where Ramco's platform fits the operational profile, where a named contact has been identified, and where the region has active sales coverage. Current SAM estimate: 150–200 accounts across four regions.

## Strategic Pillars

### Pillar 1: Win on Value Before Price Enters the Room
**The problem:** Price is the #1 loss reason. This means commercial conversations are starting before value has been established. The fix is not to lower price — it is to ensure that by the time a prospect reaches a commercial conversation, they have already calculated what their current problem is costing them.

**How:** ROI and cost-of-inaction content deployed early in the nurture sequence. Business case templates shared with internal champions. Quantified proof points from VISY Logistics and Indonesian reference accounts used in every ops and CEO-facing piece.

### Pillar 2: Integrated Platform for 3PLs — Not a Point Solution
**The differentiation:** Ramco wins on features and integration depth. Competitors (CargoWise, Oracle, Infor) are either single-module point solutions, too expensive, or too generic. Ramco is purpose-built for 3PLs with TMS, WMS, Fleet, Hub, and Billing in one platform.

**How:** All messaging anchors on the "one platform" positioning. Every email, ad, and sales asset starts from the cost and complexity of running 4–6 disconnected systems. The new TMS launch is a campaign moment to reinforce this positioning.

### Pillar 3: Australia First — Depth Over Breadth
**The logic:** Australia is the primary market. Organic rankings are already working. VISY Logistics is a referenceable AU customer. The Victorian and Queensland Trucking Association events are generating engagement. Rather than spreading a $100K budget across four regions, Australia gets the majority of investment to build a repeatable playbook that can then be applied to Indonesia and the Philippines.

**How:** AU-specific content (dangerous goods compliance guide, WMS guides already live), roundtable programme through trucking associations, Google Ads investment targeting AU logistics decision-makers, and ABM sequences built for named AU accounts.

### Pillar 4: Reference-Led Sales — Make VISY Work Harder
**The logic:** A 20% win rate with a 180+ day cycle means deals are won by proof, not promises. VISY Logistics is an underused asset. Every AU prospect should encounter a VISY reference — in email nurture, in sales decks, in ads, and in peer reference calls.

**How:** Build a VISY case study in full operational detail (before/after metrics, what systems were replaced, go-live timeline). Create a VISY-led content asset for each persona. Train the SDR and sales team to offer a VISY reference call early in the process.

## Funnel Strategy

### Acquisition
**Goal:** Generate 20 qualified opportunities per quarter from target account list contacts.

**Primary channels:**
- **ABM outbound (email + LinkedIn):** 1 dedicated outbound person targeting named accounts from the TAL. Sequences built per persona per region. Current 5% email open rate is critically below target (benchmark: 30%+). Priority fix: rewrite subject lines and opening lines using persona-specific pain language; improve sending domain authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC).
- **Organic search / SEO:** Already generating results — double down. Four gated eBooks live on ramco.com. Build AU-specific content targeting commercial-intent keywords (TMS Australia, 3PL software Australia, logistics platform ANZ).
- **Google Ads (paid search):** Allocate $25K of digital budget to high-intent search terms — TMS software Australia, 3PL warehouse management system, logistics billing software. Use Skill 09 keyword framework.
- **Industry roundtables:** Small-format events through Victorian and Queensland Trucking Association. These are working — prioritise attendance and speaking opportunities. Budget: $15K from non-digital allocation.
- **LinkedIn paid:** Retargeting website visitors and TAL account contacts. Budget: $20K. Use Skill 07 and Skill 11 frameworks.

**Ruled out:** Large-scale trade shows and exhibitions (cost/return does not suit lean budget).

**Partner channel:** HERE Maps, Hub Fleet, Macship, and WHG are active technology partners. Formalise a co-marketing programme — joint content, referral introductions, and co-presentation at roundtable events. Low-cost, high-credibility.

### Activation
**Goal:** Move inbound and outbound-sourced contacts from awareness to sales-ready within 60–90 days.

**Primary mechanism:** HubSpot email nurture sequences (Skill 04) — 3 tracks, one per persona (CIO, COO, CEO), enrolled automatically from ABM list segments. Each track uses persona-specific pain language, VISY Logistics as the AU proof point, and a clear CTA to book a demo or request documentation.

**Content offers by persona:**
- CIO / IT: Integration architecture document, implementation methodology guide, WMS implementation guide (existing gated asset)
- COO / VP Ops: Operations case study (VISY), ROI framing, dangerous goods compliance guide (existing gated asset)
- CEO: Executive one-pager (VISY ROI story), 3PL warehouse optimisation eBook (existing gated asset)

**Key activation rule:** If a contact clicks a CTA but does not convert → same-day SDR task in HubSpot. If a contact opens 3+ emails without clicking → SDR LinkedIn outreach.

### Retention
**Goal:** Protect and expand existing customer base (Indonesia: PT Cipta Krida Bahari Logistic, PT Tigaraksa Satria Tbk; Saudi: Kanoo Terminal Services; AU: VISY Logistics).

**Priority actions:**
- Quarterly business reviews with existing accounts — document measurable outcomes for use as proof points
- New TMS upgrade communications to existing customers ahead of general market launch
- Reference programme: formalise VISY, PT Cipta Krida Bahari, and Kanoo as referenceable accounts with signed approval to use in case studies and reference calls

### Revenue Expansion
**Goal:** Increase ACV from $150K floor toward $300K+ average through module expansion in existing accounts.

**Primary mechanism:** Customer success reviews identifying un-deployed modules (e.g., a WMS-only customer who could add TMS and billing). Position the integrated platform advantage as the reason to consolidate further with Ramco rather than adding a new point solution.

## Competitors

### Australia
| Competitor | Why They Win | Ramco Counter |
|------------|-------------|---------------|
| CargoWise | Deep AU market penetration, strong integrations, familiar to AU 3PLs | Purpose-built 3PL platform with tighter ops-billing integration; CargoWise strong on freight forwarding, weaker on multi-client warehousing |
| Oracle TMS | Enterprise credibility, existing ERP relationships | Oracle is expensive and requires heavy implementation; Ramco is purpose-built for 3PLs without Oracle's complexity and cost |
| Ignition / Carton Cloud / Fishbowl | Lower price point, simpler to implement | Ramco handles the full operation (TMS + WMS + Fleet + Billing) at enterprise scale; these are SMB tools that break at 3PL complexity |

### Indonesia
| Competitor | Why They Win | Ramco Counter |
|------------|-------------|---------------|
| Infor / SAP / Oracle | Incumbent ERP relationships, IT team familiarity | Ramco is purpose-built for 3PL operations; ERP vendors require heavy customisation to match 3PL workflow depth |
| Blue Yonder / Manhattan | Supply chain pedigree, global references | Enterprise price point without regional support; Ramco has live Indonesian reference accounts and local implementation capability |
| FarEye | Strong last-mile and delivery focus | Ramco covers the full operation; FarEye is a point solution for last-mile only |

### Saudi Arabia
| Competitor | Why They Win | Ramco Counter |
|------------|-------------|---------------|
| Oracle / SAP | Incumbent relationships with large Saudi enterprises | Cost and implementation complexity; Ramco offers a faster time-to-value for 3PL-specific workflows |
| Infor / Blue Yonder | Global credibility | Ramco has GCC reference (Kanoo Terminal Services); ZATCA billing compliance built in |

### Primary Competitive Advantages
1. **3PL-native platform** — TMS, WMS, Fleet, Hub, and Billing built as one system, not bolted together. No competitor covers all five in a single platform at Ramco's price point.
2. **Implementation speed** — Phased rollout methodology designed for 3PL operations. Faster time-to-value than Oracle, SAP, or Blue Yonder.
3. **Regional presence** — Live customers in AU, Indonesia, and Saudi Arabia. Local implementation and support teams. Not a global vendor parachuting in.

### Primary Competitive Weakness to Address
- **Price perception:** Positioned as expensive vs. Carton Cloud / Fishbowl in AU and vs. local vendors in Indonesia. Fix: lead with cost-of-current-problem framing before any pricing conversation. Build ROI calculators that quantify billing leakage, manual processing cost, and SLA penalties.
