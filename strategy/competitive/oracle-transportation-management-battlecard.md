## STEP 1 — Competitor Profile

### Company Overview
- **Full name:** Oracle Transportation Management (OTM)
- **Parent company:** Oracle Corporation
- **HQ:** Austin, Texas, USA
- **Oracle Corp founded:** 1977 | OTM acquired from G-Log: 2005
- **Oracle Corp revenue:** ~$53B USD annually (FY2024)
- **OTM product revenue:** Not separately disclosed; OTM is part of Oracle Supply Chain & Manufacturing Cloud suite
- **Deployment model:** Oracle Cloud (SaaS) and on-premise legacy (Oracle OTM on-premise customers still exist and are under migration pressure)

### Primary Products That Compete With Ramco
- **Oracle Transportation Management (OTM)** — enterprise TMS; freight planning, carrier management, rate management, visibility
- **Oracle Warehouse Management (OWM)** — WMS module; often sold alongside OTM in large enterprise deals
- **Oracle Fusion ERP** — the broader ecosystem Oracle uses as a lock-in mechanism; many prospects already run Oracle ERP

### Core Target Market vs Ramco
| Dimension | Oracle TM | Ramco Logistics |
|-----------|-----------|----------------|
| Primary ICP | Large enterprise shippers and 3PLs ($500M+ revenue) | 3PLs with $100M+ revenue — purpose-built for 3PL operations |
| Typical deal size | $500k–$2M+ ACV | $150k+ ACV |
| Implementation timeline | 12–24 months typical | 6–12 months typical |
| Decision driver | Usually IT / CIO-led, driven by existing Oracle ecosystem | Ops-led or CIO-led, not tied to incumbent ERP |
| Geography focus | Global — US, EU dominant; APAC and ME through SIs | APAC and Middle East as primary growth regions |

### Pricing Model
- Subscription-based (Oracle Cloud): high annual fees with complex licensing tiers
- On-premise legacy: perpetual license + annual maintenance (~20% of license cost)
- Implementation: Always SI-led — Accenture, Deloitte, Infosys, Wipro are primary implementation partners; SI fees often equal or exceed Oracle license cost

### Implementation Model
- **Always SI-led** — Oracle does not implement directly in most deals
- Large SI engagements (12–24 months) are the norm; 6-month Oracle TM implementations are rare
- Significant internal IT resource required from the customer throughout implementation
- Post-go-live: Customer often remains dependent on the SI for configuration changes

### Geographic Strength and Weakness
| Region | Strength | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Australia | **Strong** | Established customer base; large AU enterprises (mining, retail, FMCG) often on Oracle ERP, creating OTM pull-through |
| Indonesia | **Weak** | Limited SI presence; cost of Oracle TM prohibitive for most Indonesian 3PLs; local language support gaps |
| Saudi Arabia | **Medium-Strong** | Saudi Vision 2030 infrastructure projects driving enterprise tech investment; Oracle has Saudi data centre presence |
| Philippines | **Weak** | High cost and SI dependency makes Oracle impractical for most Philippine 3PLs; limited local support |

### Regional Threat Level
- **Australia: HIGH** — Oracle ERP ecosystem pull-through is real; CIOs at large AU enterprises often default to Oracle TM to stay within the ecosystem. Deals $300M+ AU 3PLs are most at risk.
- **Indonesia: LOW** — Cost, SI dependency, and limited local presence make Oracle an unlikely choice for Indonesian 3PLs in Ramco's ICP.
- **Saudi Arabia: MEDIUM** — Oracle has KSA data centre presence and Vision 2030 alignment, but deal velocity and implementation complexity work against them in mid-market 3PL deals.
- **Philippines: LOW** — Cost and lack of local SI presence make Oracle impractical for Philippine 3PLs at Ramco's target revenue tier.

### Recent News and Signals (APAC/ME)
- Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) expansion in APAC — new regions in Singapore, Australia, Saudi Arabia — improving data residency story
- Oracle pushing aggressive cloud migration for on-premise OTM customers (creating urgency conversations for legacy Oracle customers)
- Oracle continuing to bundle OTM + OWM + Fusion ERP in enterprise deals — ecosystem lock-in strategy intensifying
- SI partners (Accenture, Infosys) actively marketing Oracle Supply Chain Cloud practices in AU and SA

## STEP 2 — Win/Loss Analysis

### Where Oracle Wins (Internal AE Use Only — Know This Before Every Deal)

1. **Existing Oracle ERP ecosystem** (CIO) — If the prospect runs Oracle Fusion ERP or Oracle E-Business Suite, the CIO will default to OTM as "the safe choice." Pre-built connectors and a single vendor relationship are genuinely compelling arguments.

2. **Brand recognition and board credibility** (CEO) — "We went with Oracle" is a safe decision for a CEO to defend to a board. Oracle's global brand reduces perceived implementation risk at the executive level, even when the operational reality is very different.

3. **Global freight carrier network** (COO) — Oracle TM has pre-built integrations with hundreds of global carriers. For 3PLs with significant international freight volumes, this is a real advantage over point TMS solutions.

4. **Depth of TMS functionality for complex freight** (COO / CIO) — For very large, complex multi-modal international freight operations, Oracle TM's feature depth in rate management and freight audit is genuinely strong.

5. **Data residency and compliance in AU and SA** (CIO) — Oracle's OCI regions in Sydney and Riyadh give Oracle a credible data residency answer in two of Ramco's most important markets.

### Where Ramco Wins (Specific and Defensible)

1. **Purpose-built for 3PL multi-client operations** (COO) — Oracle TM was built for shippers, not 3PLs. Ramco is built from the ground up for multi-client 3PL operations — multi-client billing, contract rate management, customer portals, and operational configurability without SI intervention. Oracle TM requires heavy customisation to replicate standard Ramco 3PL workflows.

2. **Integrated TMS + WMS + Fleet + Hub + Billing in one platform** (COO / CIO) — Oracle TM + Oracle WMS are separate products with separate licences, separate implementations, and separate support contracts. Ramco delivers all five modules as a single integrated platform — one data model, one implementation, one vendor relationship.

3. **Implementation speed: 6–12 months vs Oracle's 12–24 months** (CEO / CIO) — Oracle TM implementations at comparable 3PLs run 12–24 months with a major SI. Ramco's structured implementation methodology delivers go-live in 6–12 months with a dedicated Ramco team — no SI dependency.

4. **No SI dependency post go-live** (CIO / COO) — Once Ramco is live, the operations team can configure new customers, new lanes, and new rate structures without a consultant. Oracle TM configuration changes almost always require SI involvement — at significant ongoing cost.

5. **Total cost of ownership is materially lower** (CEO) — Oracle TM licence + Oracle WMS licence + SI implementation fees + ongoing SI maintenance typically totals 3–5x Ramco's TCO over a 5-year period. This is a CFO-level conversation Ramco can win.

6. **Local APAC and ME implementation team** (CIO / CEO) — Ramco has in-region implementation teams in Australia, Southeast Asia, and the Middle East. Oracle implementations in these regions rely on SI partners who may not be local or logistics-specialist.

7. **Operational configurability without IT** (COO) — Ramco's ops team can configure new customers, new billing rules, and new workflows without raising an IT ticket. Oracle TM configuration requires certified Oracle consultants.

### Top 3 Ramco Differentiators vs Oracle TM

1. "Oracle TM was built for shippers. Ramco was built for 3PLs. For a multi-client logistics operation, that distinction shows up in every workflow — from billing to customer portals to fleet management."

2. "Oracle TM + Oracle WMS is two separate products, two separate implementations, two separate support relationships. Ramco is one platform. For a 3PL that runs transport, warehouse, and fleet together, that means one data model and half the integration risk."

3. "A typical Oracle TM implementation at a 3PL your size takes 18 months and requires a major SI. We go live in 6–12 months with our own team. That's 6–12 months of revenue leakage and manual processes you avoid."

## STEP 3 — Discovery Questions

Questions that naturally surface Oracle TM's weaknesses without naming Oracle.

---

**Q1:** "How are you currently handling billing for multiple customers on the same transport or warehouse operation — is that automated end-to-end, or does your team still do manual reconciliation at month-end?"

**Why this works:** Oracle TM's multi-client billing for 3PLs requires heavy customisation. If the prospect is on Oracle or evaluating it, this question surfaces the billing gap that Ramco's native multi-client billing solves. A "yes, it's still manual" answer opens the door immediately.

---

**Q2:** "When your ops team needs to onboard a new customer or set up a new lane, how long does that typically take — and how many IT resources does it require?"

**Why this works:** Oracle TM requires SI or IT resource for any significant configuration change. This question reveals the hidden cost and speed constraint that Ramco eliminates with ops-configurable workflows. Prospects often don't realise this is a pain until they're asked.

---

**Q3:** "If you look across your current systems — TMS, WMS, fleet, billing — how many separate platforms are your ops and IT teams managing today, and what does the data flow between them look like?"

**Why this works:** Oracle TM + Oracle WMS are separate products even within the Oracle ecosystem. Most prospects are managing 3–5 separate platforms. This question surfaces the integration complexity that Ramco's single-platform architecture solves — without mentioning Oracle by name.

---

**Q4:** "When you've done software implementations in the past — how did the actual timeline compare to what was promised upfront, and how much internal IT resource did it consume?"

**Why this works:** Oracle TM implementations routinely run over time and over budget. This question activates implementation trauma without Ramco having to claim anything negative about Oracle. The prospect's own experience tells the story.

---

**Q5:** "How much of your IT team's time today goes to maintaining integrations between your current logistics systems, versus building new capability?"

**Why this works:** Oracle TM in a 3PL environment typically requires ongoing SI or IT resource to maintain integrations between OTM and OWM, and between Oracle and the customer's ERP. This question quantifies the hidden maintenance cost — and sets up Ramco's single-platform answer.

## STEP 4 — Objection Handling Scripts

---

**Objection 1:**
"We already use Oracle for our ERP — it makes sense to stay in the Oracle ecosystem."
**Persona:** CIO
**Ramco response:** "That's a completely logical starting point, and Oracle TM does have pre-built ERP connectors. The question worth asking is whether OTM was actually built for a 3PL operation or for a shipper — because the multi-client billing, customer portals, and fleet management workflows your ops team needs were not Oracle's design priority. Ramco integrates cleanly with Oracle ERP via documented APIs, so you get the ERP continuity without the 3PL workflow compromise."
**Follow-up question:** "Has your IT team had a chance to look at how OTM handles multi-client billing and rate configuration specifically — or has the conversation stayed at the ERP integration level so far?"

---

**Objection 2:**
"Oracle is cheaper when you factor in our existing Oracle licences."
**Persona:** CEO / CIO
**Ramco response:** "The licence cost is one line item. The full picture includes Oracle WMS as a separate licence, SI implementation fees — which for Oracle TM at your scale typically run 18–24 months — and ongoing SI costs every time your ops team needs a configuration change. We're happy to do a side-by-side TCO comparison over five years. In most 3PL deals we've been part of, Ramco's total cost is significantly lower than Oracle TM plus SI."
**Follow-up question:** "Do you have a sense of what the implementation and ongoing support costs would look like on the Oracle side — has anyone scoped that out yet?"

---

**Objection 3:**
"Our IT team is already certified on Oracle — switching means retraining."
**Persona:** CIO
**Ramco response:** "Retraining is a real cost and worth factoring in. What's also worth factoring in is that Oracle TM configuration requires ongoing SI involvement for most 3PL operations — your team's Oracle certification doesn't eliminate that dependency. With Ramco, your ops team handles configuration directly, which reduces your IT team's support burden rather than adding to it. The transition is a one-time cost; the ongoing operational savings are recurring."
**Follow-up question:** "How much of your IT team's current time goes to supporting and configuring your existing Oracle environment versus working on new initiatives?"

---

**Objection 4:**
"Oracle has been around a lot longer and has more customers globally."
**Persona:** CEO
**Ramco response:** "Oracle has a strong global brand and a large customer base — that's accurate. The customers who chose Oracle are largely large enterprise shippers, not 3PLs. The multi-client, multi-modal 3PL operation you run is a fundamentally different use case. We can introduce you to 3PL operators at your revenue scale in your region who are live on Ramco — that conversation will be more relevant than Oracle's global customer count."
**Follow-up question:** "Would it be useful to speak to a 3PL in Australia that's been through both an Oracle evaluation and a Ramco implementation — to hear that comparison firsthand?"

---

**Objection 5:**
"Oracle told us they can do everything Ramco does."
**Persona:** CIO / COO
**Ramco response:** "Oracle TM is a capable platform for certain use cases — we'd never say otherwise. The question is whether it was designed for your specific operation. Ask Oracle to demo multi-client billing with different rate structures per customer, operational self-configuration of a new client without IT or SI involvement, and integrated fleet management in the same platform as the WMS. Those are the workflows that matter for a 3PL, and the demo will tell you what you need to know."
**Follow-up question:** "Has Oracle run a demo against your specific operational scenarios yet, or has it been more of a capability overview so far?"

## STEP 5 — Persona-Specific Positioning

### CIO / VP IT Positioning vs Oracle TM

**What to emphasise:** Integration simplicity, no ongoing SI dependency, faster implementation, single data model, Ramco's documented API approach to Oracle ERP connectivity.

**What to avoid saying:** Anything that sounds like "Oracle is bad" — CIOs respect Oracle's technical depth and will lose trust if the dismissal sounds uninformed. Stay specific and factual.

**Key phrase:** "Ramco connects to your Oracle ERP via documented APIs. You get the ERP continuity without the 3PL workflow compromise — and without an 18-month implementation."

**Full positioning:**
"For a CIO already running Oracle ERP, the question isn't whether to use Oracle — it's whether Oracle TM is the right TMS for a 3PL operation. OTM was designed for enterprise shippers, not multi-client logistics providers. Ramco integrates cleanly with Oracle Fusion and E-Business Suite via documented APIs — so your ERP stays untouched. What changes is the logistics layer: one platform for TMS, WMS, Fleet, and Billing, implemented by Ramco's own team in 6–12 months, with no SI dependency for post-go-live configuration. Your IT team owns the system. No ongoing consulting fees to make operational changes."

---

### COO / VP Ops / Head of Transport Positioning vs Oracle TM

**What to emphasise:** Built for 3PL multi-client operations, ops-configurable without IT tickets, integrated transport + warehouse + billing in one view, faster time-to-value.

**What to avoid saying:** Technical architecture details, API specs, anything that sounds like an IT conversation.

**Key phrase:** "Oracle TM was built for shippers. Ramco was built for 3PLs. Your ops team will feel that difference from day one."

**Full positioning:**
"Every 3PL operations leader we speak to who has been through an Oracle TM evaluation says the same thing: it's a powerful system, but it was built for companies that move their own freight — not for 3PLs managing multiple customers with different rate structures, different SLAs, and different billing requirements. Ramco's multi-client billing, customer portals, and fleet management are not add-ons — they're the core of what the platform was built to do. And your ops team can configure new customers and new lanes without raising an IT ticket. That's not how Oracle TM works."

---

### CEO / President / Owner Positioning vs Oracle TM

**What to emphasise:** TCO advantage over 5 years, implementation speed (time to value), competitive positioning, no SI lock-in, reference customers at comparable 3PLs.

**What to avoid saying:** Feature lists, technical specs, anything that requires the CEO to think about IT complexity.

**Key phrase:** "Oracle is the safe-sounding choice. We're the choice that gets your operation running faster, at lower total cost, and without the SI dependency that follows Oracle customers for years."

**Full positioning:**
"Oracle is a brand that's easy to defend in a board meeting. We understand that. But the CEOs we work with who've been through Oracle TM evaluations tell us the same thing: the implementation took twice as long as promised, the SI fees were equal to or greater than the licence cost, and three years later they're still calling a consultant every time they need to change a billing rule. Ramco goes live in 6–12 months with our own team, your ops team owns the configuration from day one, and the five-year TCO is materially lower. We can put you in contact with a 3PL CEO in Australia who made exactly this comparison — that call is more valuable than anything we can say."
