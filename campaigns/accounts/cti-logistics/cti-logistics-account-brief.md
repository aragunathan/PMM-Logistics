## STEP 2 — Account Brief

### Company Overview

- **Full legal name:** CTI Logistics Limited
- **Trading name:** CTI Logistics
- **ASX Ticker:** CTI
- **Headquarters:** Kewdale, Perth, Western Australia, Australia
- **Estimated annual revenue:** $300M–$400M AUD (Source: ASX-listed public financials; Confidence: High)
- **Estimated employee count:** 1,500–2,000
- **Key markets:** Western Australia (primary); national reach via interstate linehaul and courier network (SA, VIC, NSW coverage)
- **Years in operation:** 55+ years (established 1971)

### Logistics Operations Profile

- **Estimated warehouse/DC locations:** 8–12 facilities across WA (Perth metro: Kewdale, Welshpool, Canning Vale, Malaga); regional WA depots (Bunbury, Geraldton, Kalgoorlie likely); some eastern states presence
- **Fleet size:** Significant — estimated 500–800 vehicles across courier vans, rigid trucks, and linehaul semi-trailers. Fleet signals visible through WA's vast transport geography and scale of courier + freight operations
- **Key verticals served:** Retail/FMCG (Coles, Woolworths supply chains), pharmaceuticals, mining and resources supply chain, industrial, e-commerce
- **Known service lines:** Express courier (CTI Express), road freight (linehaul + metro), contract warehousing and distribution, pick-and-pack, cold chain logistics, hazardous goods transport, fleet management

### Technology Signals

- **Website signals:** ctilogistics.com positions operational capability (courier, freight, warehousing) with some customer-facing portal references. No major enterprise logistics software vendor logo or co-brand visible.
- **Job posting signals:** Review LinkedIn for postings referencing "TMS," "WMS," "Microlistics," "SAP," or "Oracle." A 55-year-old WA operator at this scale typically has a patchwork of legacy transport and warehouse systems accumulated through organic growth and acquisitions — often a mix of homegrown tools, spreadsheet-based billing, and one or more point-solution vendors that don't integrate.
- **Technology maturity rating: Moderate**
  - Reasoning: Revenue scale and operational complexity suggest the need for enterprise logistics software, but WA-market operators at this age frequently run fragmented systems — separate courier management, TMS, and WMS tools without a unified data layer. Billing complexity across multi-client, multi-modal contracts with pharmaceutical and mining customers is a high-probability manual pain point.

### Recent News and Growth Signals

- CTI Logistics has maintained consistent ASX reporting; WA's resource sector boom creates sustained demand for supply chain services in the Pilbara and Goldfields
- E-commerce growth in Perth metro (WA is underserved vs. east coast in fulfillment infrastructure) creates warehousing and last-mile demand
- WA's geographic isolation means interstate linehaul and airfreight integration is a growing operational complexity — cross-modal billing and carrier management are pressure points
- Chain of Responsibility (CoR) compliance in WA's heavy transport sector is a real regulatory driver — technology that provides audit-ready driver behaviour and vehicle data reduces legal exposure
- Labour market tightness in WA (competition from mining sector wages) puts pressure on warehouse and transport productivity — automation and system-driven workflows reduce headcount dependency

### Likely Pain Points

1. **Billing accuracy across complex multi-modal, multi-client contracts** (COO / CEO)
   CTI runs courier, freight, and warehousing for retail, pharma, and mining clients under varied rate structures. Manual or semi-manual rating and billing at this scale generates reconciliation overhead, billing disputes, and measurable revenue leakage. This is the primary financial pain.

2. **Operational visibility across a geographically dispersed WA network** (COO)
   Perth metro + Kalgoorlie + Bunbury + Geraldton + interstate means dispatchers and operations managers lack a single real-time view. Shipment status requires manual calls. Fleet location and warehouse capacity are tracked separately. Customer queries ("where is my shipment?") consume disproportionate staff time.

3. **System fragmentation limiting scalable growth** (CIO / CEO)
   A 55-year-old company with multiple acquired businesses and service lines likely runs 3–6 point solutions that don't integrate. Every new client or contract requires manual workarounds. The inability to onboard new enterprise customers quickly is a competitive disadvantage as WA retail and pharma shippers raise digital capability expectations.

### Recommended Engagement Angles

- **CIO angle:** Lead with the integration story — Ramco's single platform with documented APIs that replaces the point-solution sprawl without a multi-year IT project. Reference the data residency conversation (Australian data sovereignty) and the Chain of Responsibility audit trail capability.
- **COO angle:** Lead with operational visibility and billing accuracy — specifically the pain of managing courier, freight, and warehouse billing across multi-client WA contracts in spreadsheets or disconnected systems. "What does your billing dispute rate look like each month?" is the opener.
- **CEO angle:** Lead with the competitive positioning angle — WA retail and mining clients are raising digital expectations. CTI's ability to offer real-time customer portals, automated digital POD, and accurate billing is increasingly a contract-winning differentiator, not just an operational efficiency play.
