## Touch 3 — Resource Share + CoR Compliance Angle

**Send: Day 7–8 | Tone: Technical credibility, Australian compliance context**

> Michael — wanted to follow up with something that's come up in several conversations
> with IT directors at Australian transport and 3PL operators recently.
>
> Chain of Responsibility compliance has quietly become a data architecture problem. The
> updated CoR obligations under the Heavy Vehicle National Law require 3PLs to demonstrate
> that they had systems and processes in place to prevent fatigue, speeding, and overloading
> events — not just that drivers followed instructions. In a CoR audit, that means producing
> timestamped load records, driver event logs, consignment weights, and departure/arrival
> data on short notice.
>
> When TMS, WMS, and fleet systems are separate — even if well-integrated — that audit
> trail is often fragmented. The records exist, but assembling them takes days and relies
> on a small number of people who know where to look. That's an IT ownership risk, not
> just an ops problem.
>
> I put together a short technical overview on this: how Australian 3PLs are structuring
> their platform architecture to support CoR audit readiness without building a separate
> compliance data layer on top of an already complex system. It also covers data residency
> — where operational data needs to be hosted, and what questions to ask a vendor on this
> front.
>
> Worth sending across?
