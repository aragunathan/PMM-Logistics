>
> Something I keep hearing from operations leaders at Australian 3PLs right now — and it comes up in almost every conversation regardless of size — is the billing reconciliation problem.
>

And then someone in the billing team spends the next three to five days reconciling what the carrier charged against what the rate card says, what the customer agreed to, and what actually happened operationally. >
> For a 3PL doing $150 million in freight under management, that leakage adds up to a number that sits somewhere between uncomfortable and genuinely damaging to EBITDA.
>

## Usage Notes

**Before sending any sequence:**
1. Verify the contact's current title on LinkedIn — confirm they are still in an operations leadership role (not a recent promotion to CEO or lateral move)
2. Check HubSpot — confirm no open deal, no active sequence, no recent SDR activity on this contact
3. Scan the contact's recent LinkedIn posts — if they have posted about a specific operational challenge in the last 30 days, reference it in Touch 1 instead of the generic version
4. For Australian contacts: check if their company is a known Woolworths or Coles logistics supplier — the OTIF SLA angle in Touch 3 becomes significantly more specific and impactful
5. For Philippine contacts: check LinkedIn for SCMAP membership — the Touch 5 SCMAP reference lands harder if they are a member

**Persona-specific adjustments before sending:**

For Head of Transport (vs. COO):
- Lean heavier into fleet visibility, driver management, and freight cost-per-shipment language
- Reduce emphasis on warehouse and billing in Touch 2 — those are secondary concerns for a pure transport operations leader
- Lead with dispatch visibility, carrier rate accuracy, and proof of delivery automation

For Head of Warehousing (vs. COO):
- Lead with warehouse throughput, pick accuracy, multi-client slotting, and inbound coordination with transport
- The billing angle in Touch 3 is still relevant but frame it around outbound fulfillment billing and storage billing rather than freight rate reconciliation
- Replace the fleet/dispatch visibility language with warehouse operational visibility (stock on hand, inbound arrival, pick queue, packing throughput)

**When a contact replies at any touch:**
- Pause the sequence immediately in HubSpot
- Respond within 4 business hours
- If they express interest in a demo: confirm whether they want a transport focus, warehouse focus, or billing focus — the demo must be built around their specific operational model, not a generic walkthrough
- Update `abm_sequence_status` to "Replied" and `event_lead_temperature` if relevant

**Parallel activity:**
- All contacts in this sequence should also be in the LinkedIn paid ABM audience (see `skill-07-paid-linkedin-campaigns.md` Layer 1)
- If email address is available, enroll in Track 2 (Operations Persona) email nurture simultaneously (see `skill-04-email-nurture-sequences.md`)
- The combination of LinkedIn organic sequence + LinkedIn paid impressions + email nurture running simultaneously increases reply rate by 30–50% versus outbound alone

**Regional timing reminders:**
- Australia: Avoid contact during key AFL/NRL finals periods in Melbourne and Sydney (operations leaders follow sport closely and will check LinkedIn less); avoid Christmas shutdown mid-December to mid-January
- Indonesia: Use WhatsApp to follow up after Touch 3 if the contact has opened the message but not replied — WhatsApp follow-up is culturally normal and often more effective than a LinkedIn nudge; avoid Lebaran week entirely
- Saudi Arabia: Sunday to Thursday only; no contact Friday or Saturday; pause sequences for the two weeks around Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha; during Ramadan, shift send times to after 9pm AST when executives are working late
- Philippines: Avoid Holy Week (Semana Santa) — 4 to 5 day national pause in March or April; reduce activity from December 15 onward; if Typhoon Signal 2 or above is raised for Metro Manila, pause all active sequences until the signal is lifted
